## What the live day found

The models are not the bottleneck; idle time around them is. On one RTX PRO 6000, 6 pages took
~45 min of setup plus ~82 min of pipeline, and the GPU was busy only ~30% of the time:

| Stage | Minutes (6 pages) | GPU mean |
|---|---|---|
| Door, exemplar, ink map | 0.6 | 0% |
| Designator (Surya on CPU) | 3.4 (35-66 s/page) | 0% |
| Attestatores (3 witnesses, one page at a time) | 14.0 | 48% |
| Perlector (27B, 2 pages at once) | 23.6 | 70% |
| Recensor (quadratic manifest rebuild) | 23.8 | 0% |
| Coniector (re-copies the 52 GB weights) | 12.3 | 17% |

Also: bootstrap hashed the 85 GB model store on one core (~720 GB read); on hosts whose pod disk
is a network FUSE mount, stage hand-offs crawled through thousands of small record files (10+ min);
DAI failed the synthetic golden-page smoke by chance on two pods; two US-PA-1 PRO 6000 hosts
(driver 595.91.07, card at /dev/nvidia1) could not initialise CUDA even with the image's own torch.

## Lead decisions (recorded)

- Topology: hybrid, optimised for cheap and slow. A long-lived cheap witness pod (door ->
  attestatores) and a big card (PRO 6000 or A100) started and released per chunk for the 27B
  Perlector onward; more lanes or a bigger card can be added later.
- Storage: datasets of hundreds of GB on a RunPod Global Volume; runs pull what they need and push
  results back (network to network); local disk holds only the working set; caches kept clean.
- One public RecordGold record may be committed as the DAI smoke page (#280).

   and act when a billing card is idle > 10 min. config/spend.toml and pod_start_command.sh must
   support "no deadline" (today they require soft/hard maximums); keep an idle/stall ladder as the crash net (lead's schedule, 2026-10-07; "idle" = no GPU, CPU,
   network or run progress):
   - 15 min: warning to the lead's phone and the running session (pod, what stopped, since when);
   - 30 min: urgent, repeated pings to both;
   - 1 h: back up the run tree and reports to network storage and verify the copy;
   - 2 h: delete the pod (pod only, never a volume) and send the delete notice.
   This replaces today's single 30-minute idle delete in pod_guard.sh; any progress resets it.
   **"Working" means progressing at the expected rate, not just "some CPU in use"** (lead): on
   2026-10-07 a pod showed ~2% CPU for 40+ minutes while doing nothing useful (one core hashing,
   then a stage reading small files off a FUSE disk). The watcher therefore checks, per stage:
   - pages sealed per minute against that stage's expected rate (from the timing journal and the
     recipe's seconds-per-page), warning when it falls below half, or no page seals within a bound;
   - GPU utilisation during GPU stages (e.g. warn below 50% for 10 min while a chair is serving);
   - CPU cores busy during CPU stages against the cores available (one busy core on a 32-core pod
     is a finding, not "working");
   - disk read/write throughput, so a stall on slow storage is named as such.
   A rate below threshold enters the same ladder as idle (15 min warn ... 2 h delete) and the
   notice says which check failed. Report the per-stage rates in the run report.
6. Yes: a scheduler may start and stop pods during a run (hybrid topology), with monitoring.

  CLI or the API.

  `$RUNPOD_TCP_PORT_22` inside the pod) for commands and scp.
