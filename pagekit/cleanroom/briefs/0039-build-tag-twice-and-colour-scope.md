Seventh follow-up to brief 0023, same worktree (/home/user/verbatus-worktrees/pk-integrate, branch work/pk-integrate) and rules. First merge work/pagekit-prepare. An independent build-side reviewer checked your spec 0007 build at 191878f: defaults, padding, density, re-runs and determinism held up (with the detectors on, pages byte-identical to 2d10a14). Two things block. For each, add a failing synthetic test first, then fix. Report the head and what each test proved.

B1. A TIFF's orientation tag is applied twice. Pillow 12.3 turns a TIFF upright by its tag when the image loads and drops the tag (in its TIFF reader's load_end). So `_decode` (prepare.py around 149, `image.load()`) gets pixels already turned, `read_source` (around 205) reopens the file without loading, finds the tag, and pagekit applies it again. With one upright spread saved as TIFF with tags 1 to 8: tags 2 and 4 come out mirrored, tag 3 upside down (rescued by the orientation detector), tags 5 to 8 a quarter turn off (rescued by the detector); on a tag-6 TIFF the manifest says source_size [2000,1400] while the file stores 1400x2000, so the point maps do not lead back to the stored pixels; and tag_trust false cannot undo it. PNG and JPEG are right. Fix: read the tag before loading; after loading a TIFF (or any carrier Pillow turns on load) with tag 2 to 8, undo Pillow's transpose so the source holds the stored pixels, then apply the tag once in the chain as before. Tests: the eight-tag test repeated with TIFF and JPEG carriers as well as PNG, pixel-identical to the upright page, source_size equal to the stored size, point maps back to the stored pixels, and an untrusted TIFF taken as stored. Check whether any other place in pagekit (the crop check, measure, volume, tone, paper colour) opens a source with Pillow and could see turned pixels, and make them all agree.
B2. The colour check measures the page's whole side of the scanned frame, before the page-box crop (`_output_mode`, prepare.py around 937-949), so a coloured backdrop or colour target outside the page counts as colour on the page: a neutral page on a blue backdrop with a colour target below, run with --output-mode grey, is flagged with 19,148 mm2 of colour and kept in colour, and the flag's "where" is the whole page; the written page has no chroma above 30. With an ordinary cradle and target, a batch grey choice would be refused on every page. Fix: measure on the written page only (build the reduced copy through the page's own chain to its margin box, or mask to the page box), and report "where" only on the written page. Test: that page, plus the same with a red stamp on the paper (still flagged).

Should fix: thin coloured lines about 0.3 mm wide (pale-blue ruling at 200 to 400 dpi) are made grey with no flag, because the 3x3 opening on the 100-dpi copy removes them; and a pale colour covering more than about 1% of the paper raises the paper-noise level it is measured against and hides itself (greypage.py around 88-89). Either detect them (for example open at a size tied to the stroke width in millimetres, and estimate the paper noise from the most neutral paper rather than all paper) or flag such pages, and in any case say in the README what the colour check does not detect, including that inks less saturated than the paper (black and brown on yellow paper) are not compared with each other and may merge in grey.

Also add tests that catch these mutations, which all passed the suite: no axis swap for tags 5 to 8 in the tagged resolution, volume or measure; padding axes swapped; colour opening removed; colour margin set to 0; density ratio tolerance widened to 0.05; the photographed polygon not clipped; the split using the stored height instead of the tagged frame's; paper colour ignoring the tag. Tests with an anisotropic resolution and a tag will catch most.
